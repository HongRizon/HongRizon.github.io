Method, assumptions and source

What is being computed

A zero-drift random walk in log prices. Volatility is estimated by an exponentially weighted average of past squared log returns (λ = 0.94) and annualized with √252 for the headline figure. Each selectable forecast uses observations available at its origin.

σ²ₜ = 0.94 σ²ₜ₋₁ + 0.06 r²ₜ
Interval at step h: Pₜ exp(± z σₜ √h)

{{CALIB50}}

What the figure does not imply

This transparent reference model is not an AI model or a claim of predictive edge. A pointwise 90% interval is not a 90% guarantee for the entire path. Steps count observed records, not calendar days; market holidays are not extrapolated.

Source: Federal Reserve H.10 noon buying rates, read from the datasets/exchange-rates ↗ mirror, with FRED series DEXKOUS as the fallback. A scheduled build refreshes the snapshot after each weekly release; values are used as published, without reconciliation. No investment recommendation is made.

Implementation notes

Small by construction

A self-contained document, system fonts, and no runtime dependencies, trackers, or remote data requests. Date gaps and exact cent-valued prices use a compact, lossless encoding. The readable source dataset is included in the project files.

Work only when necessary

Historical volatility is computed once. Grid geometry is cached; chart updates are batched to one animation frame, and hidden forecast rows are not regenerated on every drag. One short reveal plays when the figure first comes into view, unless reduced motion is requested; there is no idle animation or polling.

Readable without the interaction

The document, a static chart, the baseline forecast table, and the method are available without JavaScript. Native controls provide a separate keyboard path. Appearance follows your system unless you choose a theme.

Reproducible by design

A single build regenerates the embedded data, static chart, baseline table, and snapshot labels; a scheduled job fetches each weekly release and commits the page only when the data changed. Readable source files are kept separate from the self-contained published page.

The interactive view is unavailable. The static chart, forecast table, and method remain readable.

Research

I care less about fashionable model names than about identifying what carries information, what a decision truly needs, and what a computation actually uses. I work end to end, from raw data and experimental design through implementation, inference, and the final manuscript.

  1. Multimodal market forecasting

    Combining price series, market microstructure, text, accounting information, and visual representations to study what information carries predictive value in financial markets.

  2. Decision-sufficient learning

    Studying the information and estimands a downstream decision actually requires, from point-in-time forecasting and model selection to abstention and evaluation under costs and uncertainty.

  3. Function-aware machine learning

    Studying models by what their computations actually do rather than by convenient proxies, using intervention, invariance, representation design, and function-preserving transformations to make models more interpretable and efficient.
